ECHOTYPE

Talk at 150 words a minute. Your Mac keeps up.

I prompt Claude Code all day, and typing was the slowest part of the loop. Cloud dictation swaps that for a different wait — upload, queue, transcribe, download — on every single sentence. EchoType runs the model locally: hold a key, speak, release, and the text is at your cursor in about half a second, ~430 ms of it transcription. The transcription never leaves your Mac — no round trip, no spinner, no connection required. And you can say it under your breath — this one is built to hear a whisper.

What it actually does

You hold ⌘⇧space (rebindable), say what you want, and let go. About half a second later the punctuated text is sitting at your cursor — in the terminal, the IDE, a browser field, wherever you were. A small pill shows while the mic is live, and only while you hold. Fast enough that dictating stops feeling like a detour and starts feeling like typing.

You can whisper at it. Most dictation wants announcer volume; the audio pipeline here is tuned the other way — gain-normalised, filtered, and guarded against the hallucinations quiet audio provokes — so an open-plan office at 4pm or a sleeping house at 1am doesn't mean broadcasting your prompts. A quiet mode in the menu bar switches to the largest local model, which hears whispers markedly better in exchange for a second or two of latency. This is the part nothing else does well, and I publish what I measure against a recorded test corpus.

Transcription doesn't care whether you're online. The model sits on your disk and runs on the GPU already in your Mac, so a plane, a train, a conference wifi captive portal, or an outage at somebody else's data center all make exactly zero difference to your words appearing. That stays true the day some vendor changes their pricing — dictation itself never depends on anyone's API.

Technical vocabulary survives. There's a plain-text dictionary that biases recognition: during development, "feature branch" kept coming out as "future branch" — one line in dictionary.txt fixed it permanently. Ship your own project names the same way.

Optional: AI cleanup. Off by default. Turn it on and EchoType sends the transcribed text — never audio — to OpenAI or Anthropic on your own API key, to strip the ums and resolve mid-sentence corrections ("one month ago, I mean two months ago" comes back as the version you meant). Your voice never leaves the Mac, the key lives in your Keychain, and if the network is slow, down, or the model gets creative, EchoType injects your raw words instead — a divergence guard rejects anything too different from what you actually said.

Two smaller things I care about: out of the box nothing is ever uploaded — no account, no telemetry, so proprietary code context never needs a security review (enable cleanup and exactly one thing changes: transcribed text goes to the provider you chose, on your key). And if a recording is silent or the model hallucinates its way into text you never said, EchoType suppresses it and injects nothing. A dictation tool typing words you didn't say is the worst thing it can do, so that path is guarded twice.

Every other dictation tool is racing the network on your behalf. EchoType's answer is simpler: there is no server. (The optional AI cleanup is the one feature that talks to one — somebody else's, on your key.)

Rough edges (it's a beta)

  • Apple Silicon only for now. The stack (Rust + whisper.cpp) is cross-platform by design; Windows and Linux come after the mac version earns it.
  • Whispered accuracy is good, not solved. Normal speech is ~95% for me; whispers are noticeably behind and improving each release. Quiet mode helps a lot.
  • After each beta update, macOS may need Accessibility re-granted: remove EchoType from System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility and add it back. Beta builds are re-signed every release, which quietly invalidates the old permission. The app now detects this and tells you.
  • The recording pill doesn't show over fullscreen apps (macOS window rules; dictation itself works fine there).
  • Tested where I work: Terminal, VS Code, Chrome. Slack and iTerm2 reports welcome.
  • English only, for the moment.
